# Blind-baseline audits crowd in, task-level leaks stay open

As of 2026-11-04, the generic claim that medical VQA benchmarks, including 3D CT ones, can be answered without the image is **no longer novel**. Five 2026 preprints already show it: MIRAGE, RadImageNet-VQA, MedVIGIL, an oncology 3D VQA paper and NeuroQA. NeuroQA even reports an aggregate image-free score on 3D-RAD itself (**68.6%**, +23.5 pp over random). The benchmark authors also state in the 3D-RAD paper that Task 6 supplies the prior as a label sequence and call it a "stabilizing prior". What I could not find in any verified source is the following list. It covers the per-task decomposition (Task 6 bracket carries the past-finding bit), the exact Task 5/Task 6 item identity, and a blank/zeroed-CT control on a fine-tuned 3D-RAD model. It also covers the finding that a model receiving both the earlier and current scan is insensitive to zeroing the CT. These are the defensible novelty claims for Part A, and they rest on a negative search, not a proof of absence. For Part B, mask-derived 3D CT VQA is prior art (DeepTumorVQA, RadGPT). TotalSegmentator volumes are inside M3D-LaMed's training data (M3D-Seg ID 0011, plus the 210-image M3D-RefSeg subset), so the open and useful contribution is a contamination-aware, blind-controlled audit of M3D-LaMed on a TotalSegmentator mask-derived benchmark. Every "CONFIRMED" below means the primary arXiv/Zenodo/GitHub page was opened by the research notes (via a summarising fetch tool), not that I re-opened it. Quotes should be re-checked against the PDFs before they go in the thesis.

## What is established: images are often unnecessary, including for 3D CT

The evidence that medical benchmarks leak answers through text is now broad. MIRAGE (23 Mar 2026) shows frontier models describe and answer questions about images they never received. Its B-Clean filter removes questions that three candidate models answer correctly without images, which cut MicroVQA by 77.0% (1,042 to 240) and MedXpertQA-MM by 74.3% ([MIRAGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21687)). A LoRA-tuned 3B text-only model on ReXVQA beat all frontier multimodal models, per the authors, though exact numbers appear only in a chart. RadImageNet-VQA reports text-only accuracy of 11.5-33% on VQA-RAD/SLAKE and near-random on its own set ([RadImageNet-VQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17396)), and MedVIGIL measures a no-image floor up to 20.6% on 2D cases ([MedVIGIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07919)). In 3D, a report-derived oncology benchmark finds lung CT "essentially solvable without the image" (GPT-5 blind 0.520 vs sighted 0.498 on one question family), but liver CT is not (0.159 blind vs 0.406 sighted), so the blanket "CT is solvable blind" phrasing is wrong ([oncology 3D VQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02809)). The classic foundations are Goyal et al. on language priors and balanced pairs ([VQA v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00837)) and Agrawal et al. on shifted answer priors ([VQA-CP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00377)). The blank/shuffled-image control has a direct 2D precedent in Zafar et al. (real vs blank vs shuffled images; VQA-RAD keeps about 81% of performance with blank images) ([Zafar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03437)). BrainDiff's factorial prior-report by image-identity test on brain MRI is the closest prior-ablation design ([BrainDiff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00593)). None of these is a CT-RATE-derived or 3D-RAD result, so the thesis must cite them as methodology and context, not compete with them on "blind baselines exist".

NeuroQA is the one paper that touches 3D-RAD, and it deserves careful reading because the earlier notes under-described it. Its Table 4 lists 3D-RAD (3D chest CT) with n = 29,218 closed items, random 45.1%, image-free 68.6% [68.0, 69.1], where "image-free" is the maximum of Gemini-2.5-Pro text-only accuracy and a per-template majority baseline ([NeuroQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20525)). The paper does not say which tasks make up the 29,218. I checked the arithmetic against the 3D-RAD bench sizes in the notes: Task 4 (23,472) + Task 5 (2,873) + Task 6 (2,873) = **29,218 exactly**. A mix of binary Task 4 items (50% random) and four-class Task 5/6 items (25%) gives (23,472 x 0.5 + 5,746 x 0.25) / 29,218 = **45.1%**, which matches the reported random baseline. This is my inference, not a statement in NeuroQA, but two independent numbers agree, so it is very likely correct. Its consequence is that NeuroQA's 68.6% is about 80% Task 4 (existence detection, where the notes record a near-degenerate 92% "No" prediction pattern for the project's model) and says nothing separate about Tasks 5 and 6. It also folds the leaked Task 6 into a pooled figure. The thesis should therefore cite NeuroQA as an independent aggregate, reconcile the arithmetic explicitly, and show that its own per-task numbers explain what the aggregate hides. The same NeuroQA table gives M3D-VQA an image-free 52.0% (+27.0 pp), which matters for Part B because M3D-LaMed is trained on that data ([NeuroQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20525)).

## Longitudinal benchmarks exist, but none controls how the prior is used

Chest X-ray longitudinal work is dense: MIMIC-Diff-VQA (700,703 QA pairs from 164,324 image pairs, KDD 2023 per a search result) ([Hu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11986)) and TemMed-Bench (CheXpert Plus; 2,000 VQA questions, 862 pair-selection questions) ([TemMed-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25143)). TemMed-Bench's headline "near random closed-book" is narrower than it sounds. Image-pair selection sits at 32.6-39.3% against a 33.3% chance level, but closed-book VQA spans 45.7% (Llama3.2-Vision) to 79.15% (o4-mini) against 50% chance. "Closed-book" there means no retrieved knowledge, not no images, so TemMed-Bench is not a blind baseline and must not be cited as one. For CT, the first longitudinal work arrived in 2026. CT-ΔBench (12 Aug 2026; abstract page states COLM 2026 acceptance, not cross-checked) forms CT-RATE paired-scan difference reports with a patient-level split of 2,638 train and 169 validation pairs, zero-shot Change-F1 at most 0.0175, and **M3D-LaMed-Phi-3-4B at 0.0051** ([CT-ΔBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11534)). It has no text-only, shuffle or swapped-prior condition. RadThinking (11 May 2026) labels temporal change on 20,362 CTs but reports no experiments, and ALTER (6 Aug 2026) feeds a prior CT and report into CT report generation; both were seen only in abstract-level notes ([ALTER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05615)). The 3D-RAD paper itself (NeurIPS 2025 D&B) is the only CT-RATE benchmark with a status-of-change VQA task, and its design (prior given as a binary label sequence because "current VLMs cannot directly process multiple 3D scans") is exactly what makes Task 6 leak ([3D-RAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11147)). Its fine-tuned Phi3-4B scores 49.30% on Task 5 and 74.77% on Task 6.

One loose end concerns the number 169. CT-ΔBench has 169 validation pairs and 3D-RAD has 169 volumes for each of Tasks 5 and 6. The units differ (pairs vs volumes) and the construction rules differ, so coincidence is the likelier reading, but only a case-ID comparison would settle it, and I could not confirm either way. The follow-on question is whether any paper tests that a longitudinal model actually uses the prior scan. Among sources verified, only BrainDiff does (MRI), and I found no swapped-prior or zeroed-prior test on CT.

## Part A: what is not novel, and what appears novel

Not novel: (1) that medical and 3D medical VQA can be answered without images, which MIRAGE, RadImageNet-VQA, MedVIGIL, the oncology paper and NeuroQA all show; (2) that 3D-RAD has a high image-free aggregate score, which NeuroQA reports; (3) the blank/shuffled-image diagnostic itself, whose 2D precedents are Zafar et al. and Ask4VG ([Ask4VG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01044)); (4) that Task 6 gives the prior as labels and that this helps, because the 3D-RAD authors say so qualitatively ("stabilizing prior", Appendix I; the "more robust even without fine-tuning" wording came from the local PDF and was not re-verified online); (5) that fine-tuned models score about 75% on Task 6, since the paper reports 74.77%. A reviewer who says "this is by design" is right about the design, so the thesis should frame its contribution as quantification and consequence, namely that the design makes the headline Task 6 accuracy uninformative about image use, and not as discovery that priors are supplied.

Appears novel, with no source found after the searches listed in the notes (WebSearch queries on 3D-RAD with leakage, text-only, label-sequence, blank-image and CT-RATE terms; full-text checks of NeuroQA, RadImageNet-VQA and the oncology paper, none of which mention Task 5, Task 6 or label sequences): (a) the per-task decomposition, showing the Task 6 bracket carries the past-finding bit on **16,031 of 16,031** rows, which removes two of four answer classes; (b) the exact Task 5/Task 6 item identity (identical answers on all 16,031 rows), which turns Task 5 into a built-in un-leaked twin control; (c) a blank/zeroed-CT control on a fine-tuned 3D-RAD model, with predictions essentially unchanged (test accuracy within 0.0042 on every task, exactly 0.0000 on Task 6 in the earlier audit); and (d) the specific result that a baseline receiving both the earlier and current scan stays insensitive to zeroing the CT. Result (d) is the strongest, because it removes the explanation that the model lacks the prior. It also separates two possibilities: the model ignores the scan because the text is sufficient on Task 6, or it ignores the scan even on the un-leaked Task 5 twin. The second is the more consequential claim and should be tested with mean-image, noise and mismatched-patient controls as well as zeros. Two further caveats apply. First, the paper's reported 49.30% on Task 5 sits below the 0.641 majority floor that the earlier review computed on the usable test-directory rows; that floor was a reviewer recomputation I could not re-run, so treat the comparison as UNVERIFIED and check it against the bench rows. Second, the earlier review's note that the baseline "never receives the prior scan" conflicted with the code audit. The assignment now states that the baseline receives both scans, which resolves it in favour of the code audit, but that statement still needs a loader-level confirmation before the title "prior-scan grounding" is defended. If "zeroing the CT" zeroes only the current scan or both is also worth stating explicitly.

## Part B: mask-derived CT VQA is prior art, and M3D-LaMed has seen TotalSegmentator

The generic idea of VQA generated deterministically from segmentation masks already exists. DeepTumorVQA's original release has 9,262 CTs, 17 public datasets and about 395K questions across recognition, measurement, visual reasoning and medical reasoning, and benchmarked RadFM, M3D, Merlin and CT-CHAT ([DeepTumorVQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18915)). A larger version, with 476K questions generated from organ volumes, HU values and lesion counts in masks over 43 structures, evaluates M3D-Llama2 and M3D-Phi3 as well ([DeepTumorVQA 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09679)). One discrepancy needs resolving: the notes list 2605.09679 as both a "revised version" of 2505.18915 and as a May 2026 paper, but a different arXiv ID implies a different submission, so whether these are one lineage or two papers is UNVERIFIED. RadGPT/AbdomenAtlas 3.0 turns mask data into reports (9,262 CT-mask-report triplets) ([RadGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04678)), and DeepChestVQA inside 3DMedAgent uses TotalSegmentator and VISTA3D masks partly (search-result summary only) ([3DMedAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18064)). A bare "mask-verified benchmark" claim is therefore not novel, and DeepTumorVQA must be cited as the closest work.

The data situation is what makes an M3D-LaMed audit distinctive. M3D-Seg aggregates 25 public CT segmentation datasets with TotalSegmentator as ID 0011 (1,203 scans in the paper's appendix; the HF card lists the same sources), and the authors annotated 210 TotalSegmentator images as M3D-RefSeg ([M3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00578), [M3D-Seg card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/GoodBaiBai88/M3D-Seg)). M3D-VQA and M3D-Cap come from Radiopaedia, not TotalSegmentator. A TotalSegmentator-derived benchmark therefore carries image-side contamination risk for M3D-LaMed, most directly for segmentation and positioning tasks; whether any benchmark case sits in M3D-Seg's train or test split must be checked by case ID, which no source has done. M3D's 1,203 count differs from TotalSegmentator v1 (1,204 CT exams) and v2 (1,228), which suggests v1 but is not established ([v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05868), [v2.0.1 on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/10047292)). TotalSegmentator offers anatomy only (117 CT classes in v2, CC BY 4.0, 23.6 GB), so "mask-verified" questions are limited to organ volume, laterality, presence, relative position and counts, not pathology. The exact `meta.csv` columns are only partly confirmed.

The ranking of what is new is: a whole-body 117-class TotalSegmentator question set; a seen-source versus unseen-source split for M3D-LaMed (for example a control from datasets outside M3D-Seg's 25); blind and zeroed-volume baselines on the same questions; and possibly mask-guided occlusion of organs. The training-free methods are all prior art: VCD ([2311.16922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16922)), PAI ([2407.21771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21771)), OPERA ([2311.17911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17911)) and contextual calibration ([2102.09690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09690)). Med-VCD lists 3D CT only as future work (via a secondary listing) and ARCD uses 2D region masks ([ARCD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17189)). I found no application of any of them to a 3D medical VLM, but each was a single search, so the thesis should claim "evaluation of known methods on a 3D model", not a new method. On compute, no source shows M3D-LaMed on a 16 GB T4. Checkpoints are stored in F32 (about 28 GB for Llama-2-7B, about 16 GB for Phi-3-4B), Phi-3-4B in fp16 should fit near 8 GB of weights, and 7B fp16 is tight at about 14 GB; this is arithmetic, not a measurement, and T4 lacks native bf16 ([M3D repo](https://github.com/BAAI-DCAI/M3D)).

## Conclusion

The literature moved from "do blind baselines exist" to "who decomposes them by task and mechanism". NeuroQA's 68.6% turns out to be a pooled figure over Tasks 4, 5 and 6, mostly binary Task 4, so it neither pre-empts nor tests the Task 6 bracket finding. The thesis is best framed as a mechanism-level validity audit with a built-in twin control, whose strongest evidence is the insensitivity to zeroing the CT even when both scans are supplied. That framing is robust to the reviewer reply "it's by design", because the claim concerns what the headline metric can support.

Part B is weaker on novelty and stronger on data discipline. Mask-derived VQA exists, and M3D-LaMed has trained on TotalSegmentator volumes, so the contribution is a seen/unseen-source audit with blind controls on a benchmark the model's authors did not build. The same zeroing, mean-image and mismatched-volume protocol from Part A transfers directly, which gives the thesis one method across both parts. Every novelty statement here is bounded by search coverage and should read "to my knowledge, as of 2026-11-04".

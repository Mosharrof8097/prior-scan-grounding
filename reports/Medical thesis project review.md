# Thesis audit finds leak real, claims overstated

The project's core finding holds up: every Task 6 question in 3D-RAD embeds the past-finding bit, and Tasks 5 and 6 are the same 16,031 items with 100% identical answers. A blank-CT ablation on the retrained baseline (v7, epoch 1) shows the model ignores the scan. Both results were reproduced independently from the project's own scripts and data. Several surrounding claims do not survive scrutiny. The "ceiling" numbers (0.555, 0.754, 0.739) are computed on 16,031 rows pooled across train and test directories, and 82% of those rows have no CT volume. The README's "+7.0 / +6.1 points over text-only" comes from a lost checkpoint, a different metric and n=150. The "below the floor" language is within noise. Since the last commit (`0515556`, 2026-10-17) only two files are new and untracked: the v2 proposal (2026-10-20) and a CV (2026-10-21). The 2026-10-24 timestamps on `perper ref/`, `preview_images/` and `retrain/audit_upload/` are folder touches, not new work. Against the literature the leakage audit is still unscooped at task level, but blind-baseline audits of medical VQA are now crowded, so novelty rests on the structural mechanism and the matched twin. The most valuable next steps are cheap inference-only reruns and a rewrite of the README and ceilings. The radiologist, taxonomy and detector steps should become stretch goals.

## Solid findings: the leak, the twins and the blank test

Three results are sound. First, `bracket_equals_past_bit` is true on all 16,031 Task 6 rows. The crosstab is perfectly block-structured, with no off-block cells, so the bracket removes 2 of the 4 answer classes. Second, Tasks 5 and 6 are matched pairs (16,031 of 16,031 identical answers). This is nearly tautological for a templated generator, but it is a useful built-in control: Task 5 is the un-leaked twin. The benchmark paper's own Table 2 sizes agree, with 2,873 bench and 13,158 train rows per task summing to 16,031 ([3D-RAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11147)). Third, the split is correctly patient-level. It has zero patient or volume overlap, 3,039 unique MD5s, and only 9 harmless duplicate question pairs inside train. The blank-image audit scored the epoch-1 checkpoint on full val (3,254) and test (3,459) with real and all-zero volumes. Test accuracy is unchanged within 0.0042 on every task and exactly 0.0000 on Task 6. Task 6 predictions are identical in confusion structure real versus blank, and the model never predicts class C. Closed predictions are near-degenerate, such as 92% "No" on Task 4. Gold log-probabilities shift by only about 0.0055 nats. Teacher-forced token accuracy (0.983) correctly flagged as uninformative.

What this proves is narrower than the proposal's wording. The test shows only that this checkpoint's outputs are insensitive to zeroing the input. It does not show the image is useless in principle, and a mean-image, noise or mismatched-patient control would be stronger. "Below the text-only floor" is directionally true but statistically unsupported. The test deltas are 0 to 4 items, and 95% intervals at n=238 to 340 are about ±4 to 6 points. Items are also clustered: held-out Task 5/6 covers only 14 val and 20 test volumes, one per patient, so patient-level bootstrap intervals will be wider still. "Indistinguishable from the floor" is the defensible phrase.

## Claims that are unsupported or internally inconsistent

The ceiling table is the largest problem. `information_ceilings.py` globs every Task 5/6 CSV and never filters to rows that have volumes. Its 16,031 rows are 2,873 test-directory rows (169 volumes with images) plus 13,158 train-directory rows (774 volumes with no CT). On the usable test-directory rows, the Task 5 majority floor is 0.641 and the single-volume perfect-read ceiling is 0.789; the Task 6 bracket floor is also 0.789. The published 0.555 / 0.739 / 0.754 blend two label distributions (P(past) = 0.285 versus 0.404). That understates the achievable ceiling by about 5 points. The reviewer's recomputation, not the project, produced these corrected figures, and I have not re-run them. Three further overstatements sit in the same table. "Task 5 + prior scan = 1.0" is hard-coded and requires all prior scans, not one. One prior gives 0.868 pooled and 0.956 on test, and only 12% of rows have a single prior. "A flawless reader cannot exceed 73.9%" is a conditional bound that ignores image evidence of chronicity. "Hard, assumption-free bounds" in the docstring is wrong. Roughly five different "Task 6 text-only" figures coexist (0.754, 0.807, 0.806, 0.796, 0.789) because different scripts use different 80/20 or 80/10/10 splits under the same "seed 42". The README's "patient-level 80/10/10" applies only to the manifests and training.

The de-leak verification is weak. TF-IDF's default token pattern drops single-character tokens, so the 0/1 digits are invisible. The Task 6 original and Task 5 control both score exactly 0.6332, below majority, so the learned text model never found the leak that exists. The bracket rule is also switched off by construction for the masked variants. A cheap exact-question lookup found no residual lexical leak (T-mask 0.634, T-strip 0.614 versus majority 0.641), so masking plausibly works. The committed verification does not establish it. 3D-RAD-T is a wording ablation on one task and not a new benchmark. `task6_task4_linkage.json` joined zero rows and contains NaN, so it tests nothing.

The "+7.0 / +6.1 points over text-only" claim compares unlike quantities. It pairs generation exact-match on 150 samples (±6.7 points) from a lost v6 checkpoint with floors computed on all rows, and the audit puts the pooled longitudinal accuracy near the floor (about 0.712). The README is also stale: it predates the blank-image result and still calls the gain pending. The proposal's evaluation checkpoint is epoch 1 (val loss 0.3115), not the best epoch 3 (0.2986). Epochs 2 and 3 were never audited, and the per-epoch 100-item subsamples (Task 5: 0.62, 0.64, 0.58) are too noisy to settle it.

One factual conflict remains unresolved. The literature notes say the baseline never receives the prior scan. The training audit, reading the code, says the audit's Task 5 input does pair the previous lettered scan as T0 (476 of 476 val items paired), with an ambiguous fallback that keeps whichever reconstruction comes last. Proposal v2 simultaneously treats Task 5 as a single-volume task. This must be settled by reading the loader, because the title "prior-scan grounding" depends on it. If priors were fed in, the Task 5 null result is a stronger finding. If not, the title is ahead of the evidence.

## The literature supports the audit but narrows its novelty

3D-RAD is a NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks paper built on CT-RATE. Its authors state that Task 6 supplies the prior as a label sequence and call it a "stabilizing prior" that makes the task easier even zero-shot ([3D-RAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11147)). Their fine-tuned Phi3-4B scores 74.77% on Task 6, close to the project's 75.4% text-only figure. The authors never quantify the shortcut or report a text-only floor. Reviewers will therefore say "this is by design", so the framing must be that the design makes the headline metric uninformative about image use. Hence claims such as "every published number is reported without a text-only floor" and "nobody has asked" are unverified universals and should be softened.

I found no paper that reports the Task 6 bracket leak, the Task 5/6 twin identity or a ceiling analysis. The closest overlap is NeuroQA (19 May 2026), which reports a 68.6% image-free score on 3D-RAD overall, with a Gemini text-only and per-template majority method and no task breakdown ([NeuroQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20525)). The thesis should cite it and reconcile the numbers. MIRAGE shows frontier models score highly on medical benchmarks without images and proposes B-Clean ([MIRAGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21687)). An oncology 3D VQA paper reports lung CT questions nearly solvable blind ([oncology VQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02809)). Longitudinal work is concurrent: TemMed-Bench finds many LVLMs near random closed-book ([TemMed-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25143)), and CT-ΔBench (12 Aug 2026) builds CT-RATE follow-up pairs with 169 val pairs, no text-only baseline and zero-shot Change-F1 at most 0.0175 ([CT-ΔBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11534v1)). Its 169-pair count echoes 3D-RAD's 169 Task 5/6 volumes, possibly a shared source, unverified. The local literature review covers hallucination detection and mitigation but misses all shortcut, blind-baseline and longitudinal-benchmark work, so it needs a new section. Several of these sources were seen only as snippets (RadImageNet-VQA, MedVIGIL, and the 2606.10066 contamination audit), and the notes found no 2026–27 deadline dates. Verify before citing.

## Conclusion

The project is stronger as a benchmark-validity study than as the detector-and-mitigation thesis it started as, but its sharpest numbers are the ones most in need of correction. The leak and twin findings are structural, hold at 100% on every row, and survive the audit. The headline ceilings, the +7/+6 gain and the "de-leaked" verification do not. Fixing them costs days, not months, and raises credibility more than any new experiment.

The honest scope for a B.Sc. thesis is the audit, corrected ceilings, the twin control and a properly controlled grounding test on one baseline, plus at most one repaired variant. Evidence is thin where it matters most: 14 val and 20 test temporal volumes, one seed, one epoch, no supervisor named in the files, and no radiologist. Several judgments above are the reviewers' own inferences, such as the 5-point ceiling understatement and the feasibility calls. I did not independently re-run the sklearn-based scripts (scikit-learn is not installed) or check whether the GitHub repository is public.
